Flop Texture Drill

 
 

This follows Jonathan Little's continuation betting framework from the Master The Fundamentals series on PokerCoaching, with one deliberate change, noted at the bottom.

The two questions

Frequency is set by hand types, range advantage and position.
Size is set by nut advantage, range connectivity and stack depth.

Range advantage sets frequency

EquityAdvantageWhat I bet
Over 58%Strong, bet most of the timePremium + Draw + Marginal + Junk
54–58%Moderate, bet oftenPremium + Draw + extra bluffs
Under 54%Weak, bet less oftenPremium + Draw

Premium is Stacks plus Thick Value, Draw is Draws, Marginal is Thin Value plus Showdown, Junk is Air. The bucket table in the feedback names the actual combos in each.

Position also sets frequency

Bet more often in position than out of position, and position informs range advantage in the first place through the default starting ranges. A strong advantage out of position is listed as not applicable, so every band drops one step when I'm out of position.

AdvantageIn positionOut of position
StrongNearly every flop, 80%+Not applicable, so bet often instead
ModerateOften, 40–80%Often, 25–75%
WeakLess oftenLess often

Nut advantage and connectivity set size

Nut advantage is holding more combinations of premium hands, usually two pair and better, than the opponent on this board. Have it, bet larger. Don't, bet smaller.

Little's slide says nut advantage is having more combinations of premium hands. This tool compares the share of each range instead, because raw combinations mostly just measure whose range is wider. On the same random flops, the combination count hands the nut advantage to the CO opener 100% of the time against a BTN caller and to the LJ opener only 21% of the time against the big blind, purely on range width. Share is also the number that matters for the decision: if I bet big, how often can they show up with a hand that raises me.

Range connectivity is how strongly the board hits the opponent's likely continuing range. Their range connects well, bet larger. It connects poorly, bet smaller. It only really matters once I already have the range advantage.

Over 50% equityNut advantageTheir range connectsSize
—A wash, within 10%—Small, about 1/3 pot
NoYes—Large, about 2/3 pot, polarised

Two different lines are in play and it's worth keeping them apart. The 54% and 58% lines sort the advantage into strong, moderate or weak, which sets frequency. The 50% line is the yes/no branch at the top of the sizing tree. A spot can be a weak advantage at 52% and still take the "yes" branch for sizing.

One more rule sits on top of the tree. When the two nut shares are within 10% of each other the nut advantage is a wash, and the size drops to small no matter what the rest of the tree says, because neither side can build a big pot without more of the top hands than the other.

His chart says small is under 33% and large is over 50%. Those are boundaries rather than sizes, so the drill uses about 1/3 pot and about 2/3 pot, which is what you'd actually click. Half pot is what a solver picks when the two size inputs pull opposite ways, and it isn't worth carrying as a third option here.

There is also a general relationship between the two. Bet more often, bet smaller. Bet less often, bet bigger.

Static and dynamic

Not one of his three size inputs, but he makes the same point another way: bet small on boards where the current nuts is likely to still be the nuts by the river. That's a static board. The drill shows it as supporting evidence rather than grading it.

Two things flip a board dynamic. Flush draws, which is why A72 with two spades is dynamic despite looking bone dry, and enough straight draws, which is why 976 is dynamic and 972 isn't. AKJ rainbow looks coordinated and plays static, because the ace caps the board and no two cards make an open-ender.

How the answers are computed

ItemMethod
Range advantageMonte Carlo equity of range vs range on this flop, 30,000 runouts, then bucketed at his 58% and 54% lines. Within 0.8 of the 58%, 54% or 50% line is flagged as mixed.
Nut advantageShare of each range making a set or better, a straight, a flush, or two pair using the top board card. Within 10% relative is flagged as a wash.
Range connectivityShare of the opponent's range in Stacks, Thick Value or Draws, which is the part of their range that continues. Over 33% counts as connected, 28–38% is flagged as borderline.
TextureShare of combos across both ranges flopping an open-ender or better, or a flush draw or better. Over 5% is dynamic.

The one change from his charts

His sizing tree sends every flop under 50% equity to large. This tool sends it there only when I also hold the nut advantage. With neither advantage it bets less often and small, which is the more conservative read and avoids building a pot I can't defend.

What the drill asks and why

Three reads, then the two decisions they feed. Is the board static or dynamic, whose range does it favour, who holds more of the nutted hands, then how often to bet and how big.

The two decisions aren't just the reads repeated back. Frequency takes the advantage and applies position on top, which is where a strong advantage out of position drops a band. Size takes the nut advantage and applies connectivity on top, which is the one input the drill doesn't ask for directly, so the size answer is what tests it.

The advantage question doesn't ask for a percentage, because nobody can read equity to a decimal mid-hand. Strongly mine is over 58%, slightly mine is 54–58%, even or theirs is under 54%, but the way to get there at the table is the seat matchup plus the board family. An LJ open against a big blind is strongly mine on most boards, because 15% of hands is playing against 23%. A button open against a big blind is barely mine at all, because they defend nearly as wide as I open. As the caller it's never mine. Then the board family shifts it a step.

Flop families

Every flop gets a name so boards can be learned in groups instead of one at a time. The family comes first, then the suits.

FamilyWhat it meansWho it favours
Ace-highAn ace and two low cardsThe raiser, who holds far more aces
BroadwayTwo or more cards ten or higherThe raiser, who holds more big cards
One high cardOne card ten or higher, nothing elseThe raiser, but only mildly
LowNothing above a nine, disconnectedMisses both, so the raiser leads on overcards
ConnectedEnough straight draws to matterThe caller, with more small suited and connected hands
PairedAny pair on boardNobody, whoever was ahead preflop stays ahead

Then rainbow, two-tone or monotone. Any flush draw on board makes it dynamic on its own, which is why about two flops in three come out dynamic.

Anything sitting within a hair of a threshold accepts either answer, so a coin flip is never marked as a miss. That covers roughly 40% of nut advantage reps.

Ranges are approximations of standard 100bb 6-max charts, not solver output. Edit them on the Ranges tab and every answer recomputes. Stack depth is his third size input and is not drilled here, since every spot is 100bb to start.
