The five to actually memorize

Quarter 17, third 20, half 25, three-quarters 30, pot 33. Everything else sits between two of those, so interpolate instead of calculating. A 60% pot bet is between 25 and 30, so call it 27.

Where it tops out

Overbets move the price less than they feel like they do. Going from a pot bet to a 2x pot bet only costs you 7 points, and even an infinite bet caps at 50%.

From the big blind, facing an open

Your 1bb is already in the pot, so a 3bb open costs you 2, not 3. Heads up the price is (R − 1) / (2R + 0.5).

3-bets and 4-bets

Facing someone else's 3-bet you are squeezed. BTN opens 3bb, SB 3-bets to 12bb, you call 11 into 16 for 40.7%. That is why the BB folds nearly everything there.

When you 3-bet and get 4-bet the price flips the other way. You make it 12bb, he 4-bets to 27bb, and you call 15 into 39.5 for 27.5%. Your own 12 is dead money, so the number is far better than the 27 makes it feel.

Preflop has no outs

Every number above is a floor, not a target. You are comparing to raw equity against his opening range, and out of position you realize about 85% of it. Add 4 or 5 points before you call.

When someone calls ahead of you

The chart assumes you are next to act. Every cold caller adds a bet to the pot without adding one to your cost, so your price improves. A half pot bet is 25% heads up, but 20% once a player calls in front of you. This is the most common leak in limped, multiway pots.

The math, if you need it

Price to call

Count it in the order you see it at the table. Pot in front of the dealer, plus the bet in front of him, is the pot now. Then:

call / (pot now + call)

He bets 10 into a 20 pot. Pot now is 30, you call 10, so 10 / 40 = 25%. Note the bet also tells you the sizing: 10 into 20 is half pot, and half pot is always 25%.

Two ways to get there, same answer. Count the chips when the numbers are awkward, use the sizing when they are clean.

Outs → equity

One card to come: outs × 2. Two cards to come: outs × 4, then subtract about outs − 8 once you have more than 8 outs (the 4x rule runs hot on big draws).

4 outs (gutshot)8% / 17%
8 outs (open-ender)17% / 32%
9 outs (flush draw)19% / 35%
12 outs (gutshot + flush)26% / 45%
15 outs (open-ender + flush)32% / 54%

Left number is one card to come, right is two.

Why one card, not two

On the flop you only get to see the turn for the price you're paying now. The river usually costs another bet. Use ×4 only when the money is already in the middle.

What this drill ignores

Pure price only. No implied odds, no fold equity, no blockers. Those move the answer at a real table, but you can't weigh them until the raw number is instant.

Combo draws are not just addition

Add the two pieces, then take out whatever you counted twice. The overlap is always a card that belongs to both draws:

Flush draw + gutshot9 + 4 − 1 = 12
Flush draw + pair9 + 6 − 1 = 14
Flush draw + open-ender9 + 8 − 2 = 15
Gutshot + one overcard4 + 3 − 0 = 7
Gutshot + two overcards4 + 6 − 0 = 10

Take the flush draw plus a gutshot. Nine cards of your suit fill the flush, four cards of one rank fill the straight, but one of those four is your suit and already sits inside the nine. Count it once and you have 12, count it twice and you talk yourself into a call with 13.

The open-ender version subtracts two, one card at each end. The straight and flush pieces never overlap on the overcard draws, since an overcard out is a rank you hold and the straight card is a different rank, so nothing comes out.

Why the count moves

The chart is the starting point, not the answer. Your own cards and the board change it every hand:

An overcard out is dead if it pairs him instead. A straight card that puts a third suit out there is not an out against a flush. Two of your flush outs may also be straight outs, so counting both double counts. When the board pairs, every out that fills your straight can be filling his boat.

How to count

Name the hand you are drawing to, count the cards that make it, then take away the ones that make him something better. What is left is the number you multiply by 2.
